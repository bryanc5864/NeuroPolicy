\begin{abstract}
Brain--computer interfaces (BCIs) typically decode user intent at a
fixed window and evaluate alternatives on-policy in a deterministic
simulator, with no behavior policy, no propensity, and no rigorous
off-policy machinery. We reformulate within-trial motor-imagery EEG
decoding as an offline reinforcement-learning problem with action
space $\{\textsc{Commit}_k, \textsc{Defer}, \textsc{Recal},
\textsc{Abstain}\}$, and contribute the first port of calibrated
fitted-Q evaluation (FQE) to BCI: validated against on-policy
Monte-Carlo ground truth across a 12-policy panel, FQE attains
Pearson $r\!=\!0.860$, RMSE~$=\!0.637$ within-subject. On the
canonical \bcia{} 4-class protocol our offline-RL decoder achieves
commit accuracy $86.2\!\pm\!5.8\%$ at $34.2$~bits/min~\itr{} with
\textsc{Defer} as a \emph{learned} action --- the first offline-RL
BCI decoder evaluated under standard SOTA conditions. We further
present (i) the first $N\!=\!10$ LOSO classification benchmark on
62-channel Lee2019 (commit acc $0.696\!\pm\!0.104$, $10/10$ wins,
paired Wilcoxon $p\!=\!0.001$), completing a monotonic
channel-count to LOSO-significance trend across three datasets; (ii)
the first $N\!=\!9$ LOSO multi-method OPE benchmark for BCI (FQE,
PDIS, WIS, DR), in which WIS strictly beats FQE on RMSE ($9/9$,
$p\!=\!0.002$, $3\times$ lower bias) revealing a
calibration-rank decoupling; and (iii) the first RL fine-tuning of
the LaBraM EEG foundation model, ties \eegnet{} on \bcib{}
($p\!=\!0.79$) with a subject-specific rescue effect
($\sim\!+16$pp). Code and per-experiment artifacts released under
MIT.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =========================================================================
We presented \nettype{}, an offline-RL framework for motor-imagery
BCI with a $\{\textsc{Commit}_k, \textsc{Defer}, \textsc{Recal},
\textsc{Abstain}\}$ action space, the first calibrated OPE pipeline
for BCI (\fqe{} $r\!=\!0.86$ within-subject; cross-subject
deployment rule $r\!=\!+0.83/+0.61$ across two datasets, no-regret
on $N\!=\!18$), and the first $N\!=\!9$ LOSO multi-method OPE
benchmark in which WIS strictly beats \fqe{} on RMSE ($9/9$,
$p\!=\!0.002$). Within-subject 5-seed mean reaches $95.8\%\!\pm\!1.5\%$ accuracy at
$16.4\!\pm\!1.3$~bits/min. LOSO yields $\Delta\!+\!0.21/+0.51$
on \bcib{}/\bcia{} (not significant) but
$\Delta_{\text{ret}}\!=\!+0.668$ ($10/10$ wins, $p\!=\!0.001$) on
the first published Lee2019 (62-channel) LOSO classification
benchmark --- completing a monotonic channel-count to
LOSO-significance trend.

\begin{tabular}{l r r r}

\midrule

PDIS                & $0.29 \pm 0.34$ & $1.19 \pm 0.41$ & $+1.14$ \\
\textbf{WIS}        & $0.27 \pm 0.38$ & $\mathbf{0.82 \pm 0.39}$ & $\mathbf{+0.48}$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

WIS-naive collapses to the deterministic-argmax target on the full
panel (an importance-sampling pathology). Restricting to
non-degenerate targets $\{T_{0.5}, T_{1.0}, T_{5.0}, \text{mix}\}$:
$V_{\text{WIS}}\!=\!-1.339$ vs.\ default $-1.383$, paired-bootstrap
$\Pr(\text{WIS}\!\ge\!\text{default})\!=\!0.99$, recovering $28\%$
of the gap-to-oracle. \emph{Recipe: exclude deterministic-argmax
policies from any WIS deployment set.}

\section{\fqe{} Bias and Slope-Corrected Impossibility}\label{app:fqe_bias}
% =========================================================================

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../figures/m21_fqe_bias.png}
\caption{Fitted-Q estimator shrinks toward zero on the
$N\!=\!9$~LOSO OPE corpus. Linear fit
$V_{\fqe}\!=\!0.57\,V_{\text{GT}}\!+\!0.70$ ($r\!=\!+0.62$);
weak-decodability subjects (red) exhibit $44\%$ larger shrinkage.}
\label{fig:app:fqe_bias}
\end{figure}

\fqe{} shrinks toward zero
($V_{\fqe}\!=\!0.57\,V_{\text{GT}}\!+\!0.70$, $r\!=\!+0.62$;
$44\%$ more on weak-decodability subjects, see
Fig.~\ref{fig:app:fqe_bias}). Any monotone affine
correction $\hat V = (V_{\fqe} - b)/m$ preserves within-subject
argmax over policies, so the LOSO-corrected $\Delta$ versus naive
\fqe{} on policy selection is identically zero ($+0.000$) on all 9
subjects. Subject-conditioning is therefore \emph{necessary} for any
OPE-bias correction to alter cross-subject policy selection.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{app:repro}
% =========================================================================

\textbf{Hardware}: single NVIDIA RTX 3080 (10 GB), CUDA 12.4. Total
compute: $\sim\!24$ GPU-hours.
\textbf{Software}: Python 3.11, PyTorch 2.6+cu124, MNE, MOABB,
braindecode 0.9, d3rlpy, scikit-learn.
\textbf{Datasets} (public, via MOABB): \bcib{}
(\texttt{BNCI2014\_004}), \bcia{} (\texttt{BNCI2014\_001}), Lee2019
(\texttt{Lee2019\_MI}).
\textbf{Seeds}: every $\pm$ standard deviation in the paper is over
5 seeds; data preprocessing (splits, normalisation) is deterministic.
\textbf{Code release (anonymised for blind review)}: full pipeline
--- data preprocessing, MDP simulator, agent, OPE estimators,
foundation-model integration, all $40$ experiment scripts, and
per-experiment summary JSONs --- is available at
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/NeuroPolicy-AFB8}. Every
numerical claim in the paper traces to a specific summary JSON in
the released \texttt{experiments/} directory. The de-anonymised
repository will be released under MIT at the camera-ready URL.
