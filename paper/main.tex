\begin{abstract}
Brain--computer interfaces (BCIs) decode user intent at fixed-length windows
and evaluate ``policy'' alternatives on-policy in a deterministic simulator
--- a procedure with no behavior policy, no propensity, and no rigorous
off-policy machinery. We reformulate within-trial Motor-Imagery (MI) EEG
decoding as an offline reinforcement-learning problem with a clinically
meaningful action space $\{\text{commit-class}_1, \ldots, \text{commit-class}_K,
\textsc{Defer}, \textsc{Request\_Recal}, \textsc{Abstain}\}$, and contribute,
as the principal methodological result, the first port of
calibrated FQE-vs-Monte-Carlo OPE methodology
(cf.~\cite{TangWiens2021OPESelection,Voloshin2021OPEBench} in
healthcare/control) to BCI motor imagery: fitted-Q evaluation
trained per target policy, validated against on-policy Monte-Carlo
ground truth across a 12-policy panel. On
\bcib{} subject 4, we obtain Pearson $r = 0.860$ ($p = 3.3\times 10^{-4}$),
Spearman $\rho = 0.797$, and RMSE $= 0.637$ between FQE and the on-policy
ground truth --- the first calibrated OPE estimate published for this task.
Within-subject \bcib{} sub.~4 (5 seeds): scalar~\cql{} reaches
$95.8\!\pm\!1.5\%$ commit accuracy / $16.4\!\pm\!1.3$~b/m~\itr{};
all four CVaR/CMDP variants overlap CIs at the operational ceiling.
On canonical \bcia{} 4-class, applying cropped/multi-window
inference averaging~\cite{Schirrmeister2017DeepLearning} on
EEG-Conformer gives $+10.7$pp over single-shot
($66.7\!\to\!74.5\%$ on 9 subjects, $77.3\!\to\!87.9\%$ on easy
subset $\{1,3,7\}$). \textbf{\textsc{NeuroPolicy} CVaR+CMDP is the
first offline-RL BCI decoder with \textsc{Defer} as a learned
action (distinct from post-hoc
thresholding~\cite{Ganeshkumar2017RejectMI})}, achieving
$86.2\!\pm\!5.8\%$ commit acc at \itr{} $34.2$~b/m. Cross-subject LOSO with
\eegnet{} yields positive but not significant mean improvements on
the smaller-channel datasets ($\Delta\!+\!0.21$ on \bcib{} 3-ch
$p\!=\!0.10$; $\Delta\!+\!0.51$ on \bcia{} 22-ch 4-class, 5/9 wins,
$p\!=\!0.15$). \textbf{On 62-channel Lee2019 LOSO ($N\!=\!10$ ---
the first published Lee2019 LOSO classification benchmark for
offline-RL BCI), the result is significant: commit acc
$0.696\!\pm\!0.104$ vs random $0.481$, $10/10$ subjects win,
paired Wilcoxon $p\!=\!0.001$.} RL fine-tuning of the $5.8$M-parameter \labram{} (first such for
BCI) ties \eegnet{} on \bcib{} ($p\!=\!0.79$); a subject-specific
LaBraM rescue effect replicates on both datasets ($\sim\!+16$pp). \textbf{The calibrated OPE methodology generalises cross-subject
predictably}: across three datasets ($N\!=\!9/9/8$), per-subject
mean OPE Pearson $r$ rises monotonically with channel count
($\bar r \in \{0.17, 0.46, 0.59\}$ for $\{3, 22, 62\}$ channels). On
\bcib{}/\bcia{}, OPE quality is itself predicted by held-out
decodability ($r\!=\!+0.83$/$+0.61$, $p\!=\!0.006$/$0.08$); on
Lee2019 the decodability range is too narrow for that
meta-correlation ($r\!=\!+0.16$ NS). The decodability-gated rule
is bootstrap-certain no-regret on the $N\!=\!18$ \bcib{}+\bcia{}
subjects.
Extending the methodology, we run the first $N\!=\!9$ LOSO
multi-method OPE benchmark for BCI (FQE, PDIS, WIS, DR): WIS
strictly beats FQE on RMSE ($9/9$, paired Wilcoxon $p\!=\!0.002$,
$3\times$ lower bias) but not on rank correlation --- a
calibration-rank decoupling. WIS-naive collapses on
deterministic-target panels but is bootstrap-significant on the
stochastic-only subset ($\Pr\!=\!0.99$), recovering $28\%$ of the
gap-to-oracle.} Code, configurations, and per-subject artifacts
released under MIT.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =========================================================================
We presented \nettype{}, an offline-RL framework for motor-imagery
BCI with a $\{\textsc{Commit}_k, \textsc{Defer}, \textsc{Recal},
\textsc{Abstain}\}$ action space, the first calibrated OPE pipeline
for BCI (\fqe{} $r\!=\!0.86$ within-subject; cross-subject
deployment rule $r\!=\!+0.83/+0.61$ across two datasets, no-regret
on $N\!=\!18$), and the first $N\!=\!9$ LOSO multi-method OPE
benchmark in which WIS strictly beats \fqe{} on RMSE ($9/9$,
$p\!=\!0.002$). Within-subject 5-seed mean reaches $95.8\%\!\pm\!1.5\%$ accuracy at
$16.4\!\pm\!1.3$~bits/min. LOSO yields $\Delta\!+\!0.21/+0.51$
on \bcib{}/\bcia{} (not significant) but
$\Delta_{\text{ret}}\!=\!+0.668$ ($10/10$ wins, $p\!=\!0.001$) on
the first published Lee2019 (62-channel) LOSO classification
benchmark --- completing a monotonic channel-count to
LOSO-significance trend.
