\begin{abstract}
Motor-imagery brain--computer interfaces (BCIs) traditionally
commit to a class label at a fixed window post-cue. Two
consequences follow: every trial pays the full wait cost, and the
user has no principled way to abstain when the EEG signal is
ambiguous. We reframe within-trial decoding as a sequential
decision a learned agent must make: at each $0.25$-s timestep the
agent may commit a class (\textsc{Commit}$_k$), wait for stronger
evidence (\textsc{Defer}), request a recalibration trial
(\textsc{Recal}), or abstain (\textsc{Abstain}). Training such an
agent from logged trials requires \emph{offline} reinforcement
learning; evaluating a candidate policy without re-running it on a
human user requires \emph{off-policy evaluation} (OPE) --- both
mature in clinical decision-making but, to our knowledge, unused
in BCI. This paper supplies both. We train a Conservative
Q-Learning agent on a deterministic per-trial MDP and validate
the resulting policies with a calibrated fitted-Q evaluator
against on-policy Monte-Carlo ground truth. On a 12-policy panel
(\bcib{}~subject 4), the OPE estimate tracks ground truth at
Pearson $r\!=\!0.860$ with rank ordering preserved --- the
property required for offline policy selection, and the first
such calibrated OPE estimate published for BCI motor imagery.
Under the standard \bcia{} 4-class evaluation protocol, the
trained agent reaches $86.2\!\pm\!5.8\%$ commit accuracy at
$34.2$~bits/min~\itr{} with wrong-commit rate $\le 5\%$ using
an EEGNet encoder; a drop-in EEG-Conformer encoder lifts this to
$\mathbf{87.6\!\pm\!2.9\%}$ at $\mathbf{37.0}$~b/m, with a
$+9.9$pp encoder-rescue effect concentrated on the
weakest-decoding subject (S07). The agent occupies the
high-accuracy point of the accuracy--throughput Pareto frontier
we benchmark: head-to-head against confidence-threshold EEGNet
baselines on the same data, threshold-tuned variants reach higher
\itr{} ($66$--$850$~b/m) but at much lower commit accuracy
($0.56$--$0.70$); the mandatory EEGNet baseline reaches $0.82$
accuracy at only $20.7$~b/m. The agent learns to commit only when
the encoder is confident, giving the best accuracy / wrong-rate
operating point in this regime. A monotonic electrode-count to
cross-subject-significance trend emerges across three datasets:
leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) classification on 3-channel \bcib{}
and 22-channel \bcia{} is not significant against random
(paired Wilcoxon $p\!=\!0.10$, $p\!=\!0.15$), while on 62-channel
Lee2019 all $10/10$ held-out subjects beat random
($p\!=\!0.001$) --- the first published Lee2019 LOSO benchmark for
offline-RL BCI. Against an EEGNet-fixed mandatory-commit baseline
on the same Lee2019 LOSO splits, raw commit accuracy is
statistically comparable ($0.696$ vs $0.716$, paired Wilcoxon
$p\!=\!0.43$); the selective decoder's measurable advantage is on
wrong-commit rate ($0.214$ vs $0.284$, $-25\%$, $p\!=\!0.07$),
i.e.\ a safety gain rather than an accuracy SOTA.
Two supplementary contributions: an $N\!=\!9$ multi-method OPE
benchmark in which weighted importance sampling yields $3\times$
lower bias than FQE ($9/9$ subjects, $p\!=\!0.002$), exposing a
calibration-rank decoupling that affects deployment; and the first
end-to-end RL fine-tuning of the \labram{} EEG foundation model,
which ties \eegnet{} on aggregate but lifts a previously
weakly-decoded subject by $+16$~pp on both \bcib{} and \bcia{}.
Code released under MIT.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =========================================================================
We presented \nettype{}, an offline-RL framework for motor-imagery
BCI with a $\{\textsc{Commit}_k, \textsc{Defer}, \textsc{Recal},
\textsc{Abstain}\}$ action space, the first calibrated OPE pipeline
for BCI (\fqe{} $r\!=\!0.86$ within-subject; cross-subject
deployment rule $r\!=\!+0.83/+0.61$ across two datasets, no-regret
on $N\!=\!18$), and the first $N\!=\!9$ LOSO multi-method OPE
benchmark in which WIS strictly beats \fqe{} on RMSE ($9/9$,
$p\!=\!0.002$). Within-subject 5-seed mean reaches $95.8\%\!\pm\!1.5\%$ accuracy at
$16.4\!\pm\!1.3$~bits/min. LOSO yields $\Delta\!+\!0.21/+0.51$
on \bcib{}/\bcia{} (not significant against random) but
$\Delta_{\text{ret}}\!=\!+0.668$ ($10/10$ wins, $p\!=\!0.001$
vs random) on the first published Lee2019 (62-channel) LOSO
classification benchmark, with $-25\%$ wrong-commit rate vs the
EEGNet-fixed mandatory baseline ($p\!=\!0.07$) --- completing a
monotonic channel-count to LOSO-significance trend.

of the gap-to-oracle. \emph{Recipe: exclude deterministic-argmax
policies from any WIS deployment set.}

\section{\fqe{} Bias and Slope-Corrected Impossibility}\label{app:fqe_bias}
% =========================================================================

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../figures/fqe_bias_decomposition.png}
\caption{Fitted-Q estimator shrinks toward zero on the
$N\!=\!9$~LOSO OPE corpus. Linear fit
$V_{\fqe}\!=\!0.57\,V_{\text{GT}}\!+\!0.70$ ($r\!=\!+0.62$);
weak-decodability subjects (red) exhibit $44\%$ larger shrinkage.}
\label{fig:app:fqe_bias}
\end{figure}

\fqe{} shrinks toward zero
($V_{\fqe}\!=\!0.57\,V_{\text{GT}}\!+\!0.70$, $r\!=\!+0.62$;
$44\%$ more on weak-decodability subjects, see
Fig.~\ref{fig:app:fqe_bias}). Any monotone affine
correction $\hat V = (V_{\fqe} - b)/m$ preserves within-subject
argmax over policies, so the LOSO-corrected $\Delta$ versus naive
\fqe{} on policy selection is identically zero ($+0.000$) on all 9
subjects. Subject-conditioning is therefore \emph{necessary} for any
OPE-bias correction to alter cross-subject policy selection.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{app:repro}
% =========================================================================

\textbf{Hardware}: single NVIDIA RTX 3080 (10 GB), CUDA 12.4. Total
compute: $\sim\!24$ GPU-hours.
\textbf{Software}: Python 3.11, PyTorch 2.6+cu124, MNE, MOABB,
braindecode 0.9, d3rlpy, scikit-learn.
\textbf{Datasets} (public, via MOABB): \bcib{}
(\texttt{BNCI2014\_004}), \bcia{} (\texttt{BNCI2014\_001}), Lee2019
(\texttt{Lee2019\_MI}).
\textbf{Seeds}: every $\pm$ standard deviation in the paper is over
5 seeds; data preprocessing (splits, normalisation) is deterministic.
\textbf{Code release (anonymised for blind review)}: full pipeline
--- data preprocessing, MDP simulator, agent, OPE estimators,
foundation-model integration, all $40$ experiment scripts, and
per-experiment summary JSONs --- is available at
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/NeuroPolicy-AFB8}. Every
numerical claim in the paper traces to a specific summary JSON in
the released \texttt{experiments/} directory. The de-anonymised
repository will be released under MIT at the camera-ready URL.
