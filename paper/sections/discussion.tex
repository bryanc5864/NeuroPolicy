\subsection{What worked}\label{sec:disc:worked}

\paragraph*{OPE calibration}
\fqe{} achieves Pearson $r=0.860$ vs.\ on-policy Monte-Carlo
across a 12-policy panel; rank ordering is preserved for offline
policy selection. The methodology is reusable for any
deterministic-environment BCI deployment that records full trial
recordings alongside agent actions.

\paragraph*{Within-subject selective decoding}
On \bcib{} subject~4, scalar \cql{} delivers return
$+0.232\!\pm\!0.106$, commit accuracy $95.8\%$,
$16.4$~bits/min~\itr{}, and $\Delta\bar G\!=\!+1.92$ over
random. The agent learns a speed--accuracy frontier: commit on
strong evidence, defer-then-time-out when uncertain.

\subsection{Attribution: what the RL agent adds beyond the
encoder}\label{sec:disc:attribution}

A fair question is whether the reported gains come from the RL
machinery or simply from a stronger encoder. The experimental
design separates the two. First, every same-encoder comparison
isolates the policy: \nettype{} and the \eegnet{}-fixed and
threshold baselines share the \emph{identical frozen encoder}
(\S\ref{sec:methods:baselines}), so any difference between them
is attributable to the decision policy alone. At matched encoder
the agent does \emph{not} win on raw accuracy or return
(\S\ref{sec:results:baselines}); what it adds are capabilities no
mandatory or hand-thresholded classifier exposes --- a
commit-error rate bounded by an explicit constraint (satisfied in
every reported configuration), a $25\%$ lower wrong-commit rate
at comparable accuracy on Lee2019 LOSO, a learned commit-time
distribution with a monotone speed--accuracy profile
(Fig.~\ref{fig:time_to_commit}), and \textsc{Defer}/\textsc{Abstain}
semantics with a tunable risk knob (Fig.~\ref{fig:cvar_alpha}).
Second, encoder swaps at fixed agent (Conformer, CTNet;
\S\ref{sec:results:sota}) move the commit-accuracy ceiling while
the agent recipe stays constant, and the 9-subject stratification
shows encoder validation accuracy tracks the per-subject floor.
The division of labour is therefore clean: \emph{the encoder sets
the ceiling; the RL layer determines which operating points below
that ceiling are reachable safely, and makes the choice among
them auditable offline}. Headline commit-accuracy numbers exceed
mandatory baselines partly \emph{because} the agent is selective
(commit rate $0.27$--$0.29$, not $1.00$) --- that selectivity is
the contribution, not a confound.

\subsection{What did not work}\label{sec:disc:notworked}

\paragraph*{Cross-subject encoder-agnostic plateau}
\nettype{} significantly beats CSP+LDA fixed-window LOSO
($\Delta\!=\!+0.435$, $p\!=\!0.037$) but ties EEGNet-based
baselines using the same encoder, and ties \labram{} after pooled
fine-tuning ($p\!=\!0.79$). CVaR-\cql{} is invariant to
$\alpha_{\cvar}$ on \bcib{} sub~4 (problem too easy) but
functions as a risk knob on the cross-subject panel
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cvar_alpha}). The offline-RL machinery delivers
near the encoder ceiling. Action-share across $N\!=\!88$ agents:
\textsc{Defer} $92\%$, commit $6.3\%$, timeout-\textsc{Abstain}
$1.7\%$; \textsc{Recal} and voluntary \textsc{Abstain} are latent
and would live in closed-loop deployment with micro-calibration.

\paragraph*{Task accuracy is not the right metric}
Task accuracy on \bcia{} 4-class ($0.23$--$0.45$, Table~\ref{tab:sota})
sits below mandatory baselines ($0.74$--$0.89$). This is by
design: the selective decoder is allowed to refuse, counts only
commits in its denominator, and routes refused trials to an
explicit \textsc{Defer}/\textsc{Abstain} outcome a downstream
system can handle. Compared at commit rate $1.00$ our agent
reduces to mandatory EEGNet; the contribution is the operating
points unlocked \emph{below} $1.00$, where commit accuracy reaches
$0.876$ at $37$~b/m on the 3-subject panel
($0.746$ at $24.9$~b/m on 9 subjects).

\paragraph*{LOSO significance depends on channel count}
Cross-subject LOSO vs.\ random is significant only on 62-channel
Lee2019 ($p\!=\!0.001$), not on 3-channel \bcib{} ($p\!=\!0.10$)
or 22-channel \bcia{} ($p\!=\!0.15$). The monotone trend
(Fig.~\ref{fig:channel_sig}) is the structural finding:
cross-subject MI transfer benefits from richer scalp coverage
rather than algorithmic refinement, consistent with prior
sensor-array literature. The low-channel datasets are
under-powered ($N\!=\!9$) for paired Wilcoxon at $p\!<\!0.05$ even
when the mean shift is $+0.15$.

\paragraph*{OPE rank-preservation, not point accuracy}
Within-subject \fqe{} attains $r\!=\!0.86$; cross-subject
correlations are $r\!=\!+0.83$ (\bcib) and $r\!=\!+0.61$
(\bcia). The bci2a value is weak point-wise but the
decodability-gated deployment rule
(\S\ref{sec:results:ope_loso}) is bootstrap-certain no-regret on
$N\!=\!18$ deployments because policy selection requires
\emph{rank} preservation; $r\!=\!+0.61$ still preserves the best
policy's rank on $9/9$ held-out subjects. We advocate this
estimate only for policy ranking, not absolute risk
quantification.

\paragraph*{Choice of wrong-commit budget $\epsilon\!=\!0.10$}
P300 spellers operate at $5$--$15\%$
character-error~\cite{Townsend2010P300checkerboard,
Sellers2006P300speller}, and motor-imagery BCI control of
assistive devices requires false-positive rates below $\sim\!15\%$
for end-users with motor impairment~\cite{Tonin2022WheelchairBCI,
Wolpaw2002ITR}. $\epsilon\!=\!0.10$ sits at the conservative end
of this range while keeping commit rate at
$\sim\!27$--$53\%$ rather than degenerating to permanent
abstention; the constraint is satisfied across all reported seeds
(\S\ref{sec:results:sota}).

\subsection{Limitations and future work}\label{sec:disc:limitations}

Lee2019 LOSO used a 10-subject subset; a full $N\!=\!54$ sweep
would extend statistical power. The reward function was fixed; a
sensitivity ablation across reward magnitudes was descoped. The
\fqe{}-to-oracle gap of $\sim\!0.2$ awaits pessimistic or
doubly-robust corrections.

Two encoder-side directions follow directly from our ablations.
The Song-recipe ablation (\S\ref{sec:results:sota}) showed that
stronger \emph{supervised} encoder pretraining can hurt the
downstream selective decoder --- the two stages impose competing
inductive biases when trained separately. \emph{Joint end-to-end
training of encoder and agent}, propagating the RL value gradient
into the representation, is the principled resolution and the
most promising route past the encoder ceiling identified in
\S\ref{sec:results:sota}. Complementarily, as EEG foundation
models mature beyond \labram{} (CBraMod, EEGPT, and successors),
plugging a stronger pretrained backbone into the unchanged agent
should lift the per-subject floor that currently caps the four
hard \bcia{} subjects. Finally, closed-loop deployment with a
live \textsc{Recal} action and on-the-fly OPE scoring is the
natural next step for the framework as a whole.
